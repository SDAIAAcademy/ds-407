Who does what

Four roles, and the assurer is never the analyst

Commissioner

Asks for the analysis and owns the decision it serves

Analyst

Does the work and checks it first

Assurer

Checks it independently, to the level required

Approver

Signs off that it is fit for its use

From the AQuA Book. The UK government's guide to quality analysis (2025 edition) names these four roles and requires the assurer to be independent of the analyst; the degree of separation grows with the importance of the output.

The AQuA Book is a published guide, not a Saudi rule: we borrow its structure because it is the most complete public framework for analytical assurance. In a unit of twelve, one person often holds two roles; the rule is that nobody assures or approves their own analysis.

Sources: HM Government Analysis Function, The AQuA Book (2025), glossary and section 3.

The levels

Each level names who checks, who re-runs and who signs

LevelAssurerRe-runApprover
1 Self-checkThe analyst, with the checklistBy the analystThe requester's manager
2 Peer reviewAnother analyst of the unitBy the reviewerA senior analyst
3 Independent reviewA senior analyst who took no partBy the reviewer, from the repositoryThe head of the unit
4 Second opinionA senior analyst and a second reviewerBy bothHead of unit and requesting director

Levels are defined by people and evidence, not by adjectives like "thorough". Ask: which level costs the unit most hours? Level 4 is rare by design: in next quarter's pipeline, 4 of 40 analyses (Lab 2.4).

Escalators

Two uses add a level, whatever the matrix says

Public release

The figure leaves the authority: a bulletin, a ministry indicator, a regulator's return. A published error cannot be quietly corrected.

Safety use

The result sets safety staffing (pilots on duty), a navigation limit or a safety rule. The effect of an error falls on people, not on a budget.

Personal data does not raise the level. It adds a check by the data protection officer before the output leaves the unit.

Why not raise the level for personal data? The level is about whether the answer is right; personal data is about whether it may leave at all. Mixing the two makes every HR count a level 3. Keep them separate: Lab 1.2 puts the privacy check in every level.

Safety use means the result sets who or what keeps people safe: pilots on duty, a navigation limit, a safety rule. A berth count used to set the coming month's berth windows (N1) is not a safety use.

What goes wrong. "Medium effect: a moderate impact." Two readers, two levels. Write what an error would change: "an operational decision for a month or more".

This is the evidence of outcome 1: a peer, working independently, assigns the same levels to six new requests. The usual culprit is "public": is a ministry indicator public? Is the annual statistics book? Decide and write it.

Session 2

A team review checklist

  • Adapt a published checklist to the unit's work instead of adopting it whole
  • Write each item as a question answered with evidence
  • Tie each item to the assurance levels, with a privacy and classification check in every level
02
From DS-310

Your personal checklist becomes the unit's, tied to the levels

In DS-310

Five areas, question fit to communication, scored 0 to 2 by one reviewer on one analysis.

Here

The same five areas as yes-or-no items with named evidence, each required from a level, used by twelve people the same way.

Recap only. How to score one analysis is DS-310's; this session decides what the whole unit checks, and when.

One slide of recap. If someone has not taken DS-310, the five areas are: does it answer the question, are the data handled right, does the method fit, do the claims match the evidence, will the reader act correctly.

Start from a published checklist

REFORMS: 32 items in 8 modules, written for others' work

  • Reporting standards for machine-learning-based science, by a consensus of 19 researchers
  • Modules from study goals and reproducibility to leakage and generalisability
  • Its authors say not every item fits every study: decide which are relevant

Adapt, do not adopt

Most of the unit's work is counts and comparisons. A checklist of 32 machine-learning items gets skipped on every dashboard request.

Show the REFORMS page and the checklist template briefly. Point out the template's own advice: authors and referees decide which items are relevant. That is permission to adapt. The lab gives our short paraphrase of each item, with its REFORMS id.

Sources: Kapoor et al. (2024), Science Advances; reforms.cs.princeton.edu, checklist template.

A reporting checklist assumes the paper is read by experts. The unit's readers are directors and a board: "does every number in the text match its output?" is the most common failure in the unit's log and no REFORMS item asks it.

One item

An item is a yes-or-no question with the evidence that answers it

WeakUsable
Is the work reproducible?Does it re-run from the repository, with the lock file and README alone, to the reported numbers? Evidence: the re-run log
Are the data good?Are excluded and missing records counted, with the reason for each exclusion? Evidence: a count per step
Is the comparison valid?Is the comparison fair: a baseline, a control group, the same period? Evidence: the comparison cell

The evidence column is what makes two reviewers agree: it tells them where to look. Item R1 merges REFORMS 2b to 2e into one check a reviewer actually does.

Every level

Nothing leaves the unit before the privacy and classification check

The item

Before anything leaves the unit: is it free of personal data, and is its classification marked and respected?

  • Required from level 1: a dashboard leaks as easily as a board paper
  • Classification under the NDMO Data Classification Policy
  • Personal data under the Personal Data Protection Law

The catalog requires this item in every checklist. On Day 2 it causes the biggest disagreement between two reviewers: one rates a table of vessel masters' names "minor" because it does not change the decision. The standard says it blocks release.

Checkpoint

Which item belongs in every level?

Your checklist must stay short at level 1, the self-check that most dashboard requests get. Which of these items is required from level 1?

The output is free of personal data and its classification is marked
The model is tested on data it never saw
A reviewer re-runs it from the repository
Every number has an interval
Why. The harm of personal data leaving the unit does not depend on how important the analysis is, so the check cannot wait for a higher level.

Answer: the privacy item. Some will argue for the re-run at level 1: the analyst's own re-run is part of the self-check, but the independent re-run is level 2.

Session 3

Reproducibility as a team rule

  • Re-run an analysis from its repository before reading its conclusions
  • Classify reproduction failures by kind
  • Write repository rules that make a re-run routine
03
A team rule

From level 2, the reviewer re-runs the analysis before reading it

1 · Clonethe repository, nothing else
→
2 · Follow the READMEexactly as written
→
3 · Logeach failure, its kind, the fix
→
4 · Comparethe figure with the report
Recap from DS-310. Re-run first, so the prose does not set your expectations. Here it becomes a rule with a log as its evidence.

The new idea is the log: one re-run catches one analysis; a year of logs shows which failures recur in the unit, and the repository rules follow from them.

The sixth failure

It ran, and gave 5.18 days against the reported 4.60

imports = boxes[boxes.move_type != "export"]
  • The filter keeps transshipment with imports
  • The report removed transshipment by hand, in a spreadsheet
  • No file records that step: only the gap shows it
  • Filter on imports in code: 4.60, reproduced
What goes wrong. The manual step is the dangerous one: no error, a plausible number, and a reviewer who reads before re-running never sees it.

The gap is 0.58 days. Ask: which figure was right? The report's, by luck: the hand edit was correct, but nobody could have known. A manual step makes a right answer unverifiable.

Rules from the log

One repository rule for each kind of failure

Paths and inputs

Relative paths only; every input in the repository or named with its owner

Environment

A lock file (uv.lock) and the one command that installs from it

Determinism

A fixed seed in code; no step outside the code

These become the unit's repository rules, checked by item R1 of the checklist. They are cheap for the analyst and save the reviewer an hour per analysis.

The cost

A re-run is the cheapest check that finds the most expensive errors

  • With the rules followed, a re-run is one command and a comparison
  • Without them, it is the 25 minutes of Lab 1.3
  • The rules move the cost from the reviewer to the analyst, where it is smallest

Your call as head of unit

A repository that does not re-run goes back to its analyst before the review starts. The review clock starts when it runs.

This is a management decision, not a technical one. Expect pushback ("we do not have time"): the answer is that review time is spent anyway, just later and on the wrong things.

Session 4

Rules for AI-assisted analysis

  • Turn the national generative AI guidelines into rules for analytical work
  • Specify the process evidence each analyst keeps
  • Test a rule set on real cases before adopting it
04
Why rules now

When an assistant drafts the analysis, the analyst's duty does not move

  • Musawwada drafts code, numbers and summaries in seconds
  • A fluent draft looks reviewed; it is not
  • The national guidelines put the duty to verify on the user

What a unit needs

Rules an analyst can follow before the deadline and a reviewer can check from the files.

The SDAIA Generative AI Guidelines for Government state that results should be human-reviewed and that users verify outputs, especially for official reports. The rules turn "verify" into who does what and what record shows it.

Sources: SDAIA, Generative AI Guidelines for Government, scope and Appendix 2.

The rule set

Verify, disclose, keep, and what data may go in

Verify

Re-run every assisted number in your own code before it enters a draft

Disclose

A line on the deliverable: which tool, which parts, who verified

Keep

Prompts, outputs and a verification record in the repository

Data

Public or anonymised data only, approved tools only

Each card is one or two rules in the reference set (V1 to V3, D1 and D2, K1 and K2, C1 and C2). The "keep" rules are the process evidence the catalog asks for: an auditor on Day 2 checks them.

Checkable

A rule a reviewer cannot check from the files is a wish

A wishA rule
Use the assistant responsiblyRe-run every number it produced, in your own code, before it enters a draft
Be transparent about AI useEvery deliverable with assisted content carries a disclosure line: tool, parts, verifier
Protect sensitive data where possibleOnly public or anonymised data goes to a hosted assistant

The lab's problems check flags vague words ("be careful", "where possible") and rules with no record behind them. One page: at most 350 words. The reference set is 176.

Test before adopting

Eight scenarios from the unit decide whether the rules work

Allowed

Explaining an error message; the approved tool on public statistics, prompts kept

Needs action

Code checked by hand but no record kept; an analyst who never works unaided

Breach

A pilot roster pasted in; no disclosure on a board paper; an unchecked 18%; an unapproved tool

A scenario your rules cannot decide shows a gap. The usual gap is S7: nothing in a verify-disclose-keep set covers an analyst who has lost the skill to check. That is the stretch: an unaided-practice rule.

Keeping the skill

Who cannot do the work unaided cannot tell when the draft is wrong

  • Verification needs the skill the assistant replaces
  • One analysis a quarter without an assistant, reviewed like any other
  • The supervised check of this course is AI-off for the same reason
Day 2. The same idea returns in development plans: every plan keeps unaided practice in the work.

This is a rule about people, not tools. Expect the objection that it wastes time: one analysis a quarter is a small price for reviewers who can still spot an assistant's wrong number.

Checkpoint

An approved assistant and a restricted file

An analyst pastes the night pilot roster, with pilots' names and shifts, into the authority's approved hosted assistant to find fatigue risks. She plans to disclose it and re-run every number. Under the rules, what is it?

Allowed: the tool is approved
A breach: restricted data may not go to the assistant
Allowed once the deliverable is disclosed
Needs action: re-run the numbers first
Why. Names and shifts of staff are not public data; the guidelines forbid entering data classified restricted or higher, so no later step makes it allowed.

Answer: a breach (scenario S2 of the lab). The anonymised alternative is allowed: the data protection officer's rule decides what anonymised means.
