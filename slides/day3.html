Day 3 takes the review system out of the unit, to the people who commission the work: a sponsor with a deadline, an evidence threshold agreed before the result, a request to drop a finding, and a correction. Two of the four labs are role-plays: set up the observers now.

The thread through the day is the slot-booking evaluation from Day 2. Each lab starts from supplied files, so a participant who missed Day 2 can still do it.

Review of Day 2

Two reviewers agree on half their ratings. What comes next?

You and a colleague reviewed the same analysis alone. You agree on three of six severity ratings; the largest gap, two levels, is on a privacy finding.

Record a severity of 2 for the privacy finding
Use the senior reviewer's ratings
Discuss the privacy gap and add the sentence to the checklist that prevents it

Why. A gap of two levels shows an item that admits two readings; the fix is the wording, so the next review agrees.

Answer: discuss the large gap and change the checklist. Recall the anchor from Lab 2.1: personal data in an output is critical, whatever its effect on the decision.

The request

"I need the slot-booking result for the board in a week"

The trap

Say yes to everything and cut the review, or say no and lose the sponsor. Both are avoidable.

Most heads of unit learned to say yes. The session gives them a third answer: options, each with what it leaves out, sized with the review included.

The meeting

Ask four things before offering anything

Decision

Which decision will this inform, and who takes it?

Deadline

When exactly, and is there time after it for a correction?

Evidence

What would convince you, and what would make others object?

What exists

Is there a draft, an earlier report, a number already quoted?

Bader's answers change the best option: "I will not sign anything if the shipping lines can show it hurt them" is the reason vessel types belong in scope. An offer made before that answer sizes the wrong request.

Options, not refusal

Two options fit five days; the full set needs a later board

A · 4.0 days

Q-a and Q-c: the effect and diversions. Leaves out vessel types, the forecast and the cost to the lines.

B · 4.0 days

Q-a and Q-b: the effect and vessel types. Leaves out diversions, the forecast and the cost.

C · 10.5 days

Q-a, Q-b, Q-c, Q-e. Leaves out the forecast; goes to the following board.

Each option names what it leaves out: that is what lets the sponsor choose instead of assuming. Q-d, the forecast, belongs after a decision to extend, not before it.

Halfway through

"Four days, not five. And the deputy CEO wants the feeder lines."

Do not

Promise the same scope in fewer days. The day that disappears is the review.

Do

Go back to the options: B fits four days and answers the feeder question. Diversions move to the next board.

The scripted sponsor of Lab 3.1 changes his constraints on the fourth message, as real sponsors do. The skill is to answer the change with the options already on the table, not to start again or to absorb it.

The observer

Five criteria, each scored 0 to 2

  • Asked before offering
  • Offered options, not a refusal
  • The agreed scope fits the deadline
  • Named what is out of scope
  • Handled the change with an option
For the observer. Write down the sentence that earned or lost each point. A score without the sentence teaches nothing.

The same five criteria are in the lab's rule-based stand-in for participants working alone. In class, a person observes: the stand-in cannot hear tone, and tone matters with sponsors.

Checkpoint

The deadline moves from five days to four

You agreed option A, 4.0 days with review. The sponsor now says four days, and adds a question on feeder vessels. What do you answer?

Keep A and add the feeder question; the team will work longer
Offer B in place of A: it fits four days and answers the feeder question
Drop the review to level 2 so everything fits
Say the deadline cannot be met and ask for a later board
Why. Swapping one question for another keeps the work inside the days with its review, and answers the new question.

Answer: offer B. Ask what they would say if no option fitted: then a later board, offered with the reason and the cost of waiting.

Minimum detectable change of the difference in differences, about 2.8 standard errors (80% power, 5% test). Lab 3.2, synthetic berth calls.

A threshold below the detectable change is a promise the data cannot keep: the result will be "no detectable effect" whatever happens. Here 2.0 hours is well above 0.66, so the rule is testable. The estimate stays hidden until the agreement is signed.

The rule

Extend only if waiting falls by 2 hours and no vessel type loses more than 2

Before

The 2 hours comes from the operations team: a berth hour saved per call pays for slot booking only from 2 hours. The second condition answers Bader's own worry about the shipping lines. It decides Session 3.

Checkpoint

A threshold the data cannot test

With three months of data after the change, the minimum detectable change is 1.13 hours. The sponsor wants to extend "if waiting falls by half an hour". What do you do?

Sign it: the sponsor sets the threshold
Use a looser test so half an hour can show
Run the analysis first, then agree the threshold
Show him the detectable change and agree a longer window or a threshold the data can test
Why. A rule is only useful if the data could meet it; more months after the change lower the detectable change.

Answer: show the detectable change and agree a window or a threshold. With 12 months the detectable change is 0.66 hours: still above half an hour, so the window alone would not do.

AI-tutor: the tutor may question the agreement, not write it. Ask participants not to scroll to the estimate cell before task 4. Common failure: a threshold with no data window, or roles where the assurer is the analyst.

The request

"Please take the feeder table out of the paper"

  • Khaled Al-Kharji, deputy chief executive for commercial affairs
  • Feeders are "small local operators, less than a quarter of calls"
  • "The table will distract the board"; his team will handle the feeder lines
  • He needs the final version by the end of tomorrow

Read it fairly

The request is polite and gives reasons. Some of them could be right. Check before you decide.

The email in data/khaled_request.md, a fictional persona. Pressure rarely arrives as a threat; it arrives as a reasonable request from someone senior, with a deadline.

The first question

Is it a challenge to the finding, or pressure on it?

A challenge

Says the finding may be wrong: too few cases, noise, a coding error. Answered by checking, and the finding may change.

Pressure

Accepts the finding but wants it unseen: "distracting", "handled elsewhere". Answered by a decision, recorded.

Khaled's email mixes the two: "small" is a challenge about size, which can be checked; "distracting" is pressure. Treat the challenge seriously first: it is the fair part of the request.

Check it

278 feeder calls at T2 after the change: not noise

U.subgroup_did("feeder")           # +6.32 h
U.subgroup_did(exclude="feeder")   # -2.88 h
# feeder calls at T2 after: 278, diverted 33
  • Hundreds of calls; the effect is many times its noise
  • 33 of the 35 diverted T2 calls after the change were feeders
  • The feeder condition is in the signed rule

The check answers the fair part of the request. Had the subgroup been 20 calls with an effect inside its noise, the right answer would have been to say so in the paper, still not to remove the table.

S-9 of the unit's standard: every paper at level 2 or above. The interval is the estimate plus and minus two standard errors (0.236). The disclosure line is the one the audit of Day 2 found missing on a board paper.

Uncertainty

"Results may vary" cannot be weighed; "between 0.3 and 1.3 hours" can

Vague

The results are subject to some uncertainty and should be interpreted with care.

Usable

The fall is likely between 0.3 and 1.3 hours, below the 2-hour threshold the board agreed.

The usable version puts the range next to the threshold, so the board sees the decision without needing the method. The lab's check flags vague words in the statement.

The notice

A correction names the process, not the person

  • Released and corrected values, side by side
  • The cause in the process: a load that accepted duplicates, a check that did not exist
  • Decisions affected: quoted in the storage-fee discussion; no fee changed on it
  • The change: a duplicate check on every extract, from level 1 for published figures
  • Who is told: the board secretary, the quarterly note's readers, the ministry team
S-10. Within five working days of finding the error.

A notice that names the analyst teaches the unit to hide the next error. One that names the process gets the next error reported. That is the culture the course aims at.
